The investment case in one page

Steady is not a meditation app with a trauma skin. It is a deterministic, clinician-ratified care engine that decides — every single day, per member — what is safe for that person to do, and then does it with them. The content is inseparable from the engine that sequences it. That is the asset.

Six reasons this is a good investment

  1. The hard part is already built and clinically ratified. Two independent licensed psychologists reviewed and signed off the deterministic safety configuration on 22 July 2026. Competitors sell content anyone can license; Steady sells a governed decision system that took a clinical corpus, a rules engine, and a signed review to produce.
  2. Full product is live on two platforms today. Web and native iOS, sharing one backend, one memory bank, one safety engine. A member's calm place, triggers, and progress follow them across devices. Not a roadmap — running now.
  3. The pricing ladder is engineered, not guessed. Entry at $6.99 undercuts every major competitor ($9–15) to buy cheap top-of-funnel; the ladder climbs to $34.99 for an autonomous care tier none of them offer. Every new member starts on a 7-day Premium trial — they experience the top of the ladder before they ever pay.
  4. Monetization compounds without new acquisition spend. A memory-driven upsell engine watches each member's own data and recommends a tier change only when their behavior earns it. Revenue expansion is a product feature, not a marketing campaign.
  5. Structural retention. The daily layer (breathwork, meditation, movement, sleep) creates habit; the memory bank creates switching cost that compounds with every session. Leaving Steady means abandoning a system that knows what grounds you at 2 a.m.
  6. Defensible regulatory posture, deliberately chosen. Steady launches in the wellness lane — no diagnosis, no treatment claims — with a tamper-evident audit chain, a clinician review console, and a documented sign-off ledger already in place. Most consumer mental-health companies retrofit governance after an incident. This one started there.

Section 2

The problem, and the gap in the market

For the person

EMDR is one of the most strongly evidenced treatments for PTSD — recommended in frontline clinical guidelines from the World Health Organization, the UK's NICE, and the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. But access is brutal: in-office sessions run $150–350 an hour, waitlists run months, and the format requires telling a stranger the worst thing that ever happened to you. Most people who would benefit never start.

Meanwhile the apps they can afford — Calm, Headspace, Insight Timer — offer general relaxation content with no clinical structure, no screening, no pacing, and no idea whether today is a safe day for that particular person to go anywhere near a difficult memory.

For the market

The category has split into two poles, and the middle is empty:

CategoryExamplesTypical priceWhat's missing
Content librariesCalm, Headspace, Insight Timer, Balance$9–15/moNo clinical structure, no screening, no per-day safety decision, no accountability. Content is licensable — no moat.
Coaching / therapy marketplacesBetterHelp, Talkspace, Sanvello (coaching tiers)$60–400/moHuman-capacity-bound, so margins compress with scale; expensive; still waitlisted.
The gap Steady occupies—$6.99–34.99/moSoftware-margin economics with clinical structure: screening, daily gating, deterministic safety rules, specialist oversight, and an autonomous care loop.

The one-sentence positioning

Calm helps you relax. A therapist helps you heal but costs $200 an hour and has a three-month waitlist. Steady runs a structured, clinician-governed trauma program with you — every day — for less than a streaming subscription.

Why the timing is right

  • Consumer price ceiling has been established — a decade of Calm and Headspace taught a mass market to pay a monthly subscription for mental wellbeing. Steady does not have to create that behavior, only redirect it.
  • Deterministic AI safety is now a requirement, not a differentiator — the market and regulators have turned sharply against "LLM as therapist." Steady's architecture, where the model is structurally prevented from making any safety decision, is the posture the category is being forced toward.
  • Remote EMDR has published RCT support — including a multisite randomized controlled trial across 10 hospitals showing large reductions in PTSD, anxiety, and depression delivered fully remotely.

The safety invariant that makes Autopilot defensible

Autopilot only ever narrows a day. It can make a day gentler; it can never make one riskier. Every session it proposes is validated against the same 14-step gate the member would hit manually, crisis days compose a support-only plan, and no model call composes any plan — it is fully deterministic and reproducible. This is the difference between "AI that decides your therapy" (uninvestable) and "automation of clinician-validated rules" (defensible).

5.2 The four defensible moats

1. The engine, not the content — and they are inseparable

A competitor can license 500 meditations tomorrow. What they cannot quickly obtain is a deterministic rules engine that has been reviewed and signed by licensed psychologists, with a documented conflict-resolution ledger and an auditable decision trail. In Steady, the content is worthless without the engine that decides which content this person gets today. That pairing took a clinical corpus, a rules architecture, and a signed clinical review.

2. Compounding memory as switching cost

Every conversation, trigger, calm place, and session outcome deepens a personal model the member can see, edit, and delete. Month 12 is meaningfully better than month 1 — and starting over elsewhere means starting from zero. This is the same retention dynamic that makes a personal CRM sticky, applied to something far more personal.

3. Structural AI safety — increasingly the licence to operate

The companion is architecturally prevented from making, reversing, or clearing any safety decision. A deterministic crisis filter runs before every model call. An output guard blocks a documented list of never-say outputs (simulated feelings, asserted internal states, diagnosis, cure claims, false monitoring claims, dependency-inducing language). As the category faces regulatory pressure over LLM-as-therapist, this architecture converts from cost centre to moat.

4. Cost structure

Twenty guided practices and seven lessons are deterministic text, narrated on-device. There is no per-play content cost, no CDN video bill, and no studio required to expand the library — a new practice is a writing session plus a clinician review. Competitors carry production and licensing costs that scale with catalogue size; Steady's marginal content cost is approximately zero.

The question every investor will ask: "Why won't Calm just add EMDR?"

They would need to: commission a clinical corpus; build a deterministic safety engine; obtain independent licensed-clinician ratification; accept liability for adverse events in trauma processing; add clinical screening that turns away paying customers (Steady auto-refunds anyone the screener excludes); and staff clinician oversight. That is a different company with a different risk appetite and a different regulatory posture — and it directly threatens the mass-market, low-liability positioning that makes their business work. The rational move for an incumbent is to acquire, which is itself the exit thesis.

6.2 The trial mechanic — trial the top, then descend

Every new member starts with 7 days of Premium, free — regardless of which tier they select for billing. This is the single most important design decision in the monetization model, and it is already built.

  • Every member experiences Autopilot, unlimited companion, and the full program before they pay anything.
  • Members who fall back to Base or Plus have a concrete memory of what they gave up — the upsell references their own experience, not a feature list.
  • The lower tiers are not a downgrade path; they are a retention floor. A member who would have churned at $34.99 stays at $6.99, remains in the funnel, and keeps building memory that makes upgrading more attractive later.

Section 7

The growth engine: low CAC, high retention, expanding ARPU

The strategy is one loop with three reinforcing stages. Acquire cheaply at the bottom, retain structurally in the middle, expand revenue from behavior at the top.

7.1 Stage one — the entry tier lowers acquisition cost

  • Price is the first CAC lever. At $6.99, Steady is the cheapest credible option in the category — roughly half of Calm and Headspace. Lower price means higher landing-page conversion, which directly divides blended CAC. The same ad spend buys more members.
  • The free Premium week removes the remaining risk. The offer is not "start a trial" but "have all of it for a week, then pay as little as $6.99." That is one of the lowest friction propositions in consumer health.
  • Cheap tiers make paid acquisition mathematically viable earlier. A $6.99 entry with strong retention can support paid channels that a $34.99-only product cannot, because the payback period is driven by retention, not by first-month revenue.
  • Non-paid channels the product itself generates: the psychoeducation lessons are genuine SEO surface area on high-intent searches ("window of tolerance," "why do I get triggered"); the always-free crisis and grounding pages attract high-intent traffic and build the trust the category badly lacks; and clinicians recommending Steady as homework is a zero-CAC channel that the console is already built to serve.

7.2 Stage two — the daily layer drives retention

This is why the practice suite matters commercially, not just clinically. A trauma program alone is episodic — people engage in bursts and lapse. A daily practice suite is a habit.

  • Daily reasons to open the app — check-in, a two-minute breath, a wind-down at bedtime. Sleep content in particular creates a nightly ritual.
  • Titration removes the failure mode. On a hard day, the app doesn't ask for something the member can't give — it offers something gentler. The most common churn moment in mental-health apps (feeling like you failed the app) is designed out.
  • Memory compounds. Month 6 is better than month 1. Churn means losing it.
  • Autopilot removes the decision. For Premium members the plan is already made — the highest-friction moment in any wellness app ("what should I do today?") is eliminated.
  • No dark patterns, deliberately. No streaks, no guilt, no manufactured scarcity — these are explicitly banned in the safety rules. In trauma care, pressure tactics cause harm and churn; the trust this buys is the retention strategy.

7.3 Stage three — the upsell expands monetization

Once a member is retained cheaply, revenue growth requires no additional acquisition spend. The upsell engine converts usage into ARPU:

  • A Base → Plus upgrade is a 2.9× revenue increase on an already-acquired member.
  • A Base → Premium upgrade is a 5.0× increase.
  • Marginal CAC on these upgrades is zero — the trigger is the member's own behavior.

Because the recommendation is earned and specific ("you've named a few triggers you keep running into — that's exactly what the guided program works through"), it converts better than a generic paywall and does not damage trust when declined.

The mechanic to put on the slide

Most subscription businesses fight churn to protect a fixed ARPU. Steady inverts it: acquire at a price low enough that retention is easy, then let ARPU rise over the member's lifetime as the product earns it. Lifetime value grows with tenure rather than decaying — and the upsell trigger (a member reaching for support more than once a week) is also the clearest signal that the product is working for them. Commercial interest and clinical interest point the same direction.

10.2 Design direction

  • Palette: ground #2f3a33, olive #545e53, sage #a8b8a1, sage-deep #93a58b, ivory #f7f1e8, linen #fbf8f2, moss #dce4d4. Accents: support/red #9a4f42 (crisis only), pause/amber #d7a85f, safe/green #7fa37b.
  • Type: serif for headlines (the product uses Cormorant Garamond), clean sans for body. Generous whitespace — calm is the brand.
  • Tone: calm conviction. No hype adjectives, no exclamation marks, no stock photos of people staring wistfully out of windows. The restraint is the credibility.
  • Screenshots to feature: the Autopilot plan card on the dashboard; the SOS panel; the breathing pacer mid-practice; the clinician review console; the check-in routing a hard day to grounding.

10.3 Language rules — non-negotiable

  • Never use: cure, heal, treats PTSD, clinically proven, AI therapist, guaranteed results. These are blocked in the product by an automated CI check and must not appear in the deck either.
  • Always say: "built on the EMDR method," "self-guided wellness program," "not therapy, not medical care." Research claims describe the method, never Steady's own outcomes.
  • Never present the unit-economics model as achieved performance, or imply users exist. "Pre-revenue, built and clinically ratified" is a stronger and safer story than a vague implication of traction.
  • Always note that autonomous reprocessing and full autonomous governance remain gated pending validation — the discipline is a selling point to any investor who has seen a digital-health blow-up.
